# Clarity review (Reviewer 2: reader usefulness, clarity, provenance) — commit 172c616

**Verdict: APPROVE WITH CHANGES**

The first screen works: name, "the Odyssey and sixteen more Greek works", Greek beside translations, notes, pictures, and the free/paid line are all visible above the fold on desktop and phone. Odyssey-only features are mostly labelled. The changes below fix one number mismatch and a few places where the scope is unclear.

## Required

1. **Picture count does not add up.** The hero strip, the gallery heading and the get page say "565 pictures beside the text". The gallery body says the Odyssey has "258 distinct works" and "The library has 298 pictures of its own". 258 + 298 = 556. A careful reader will notice. Use one number in all places, or say what the extra 9 are. (The accuracy reviewer should confirm which number is right.)

2. **"58 translations · the Odyssey in 11 languages"** (translations eyebrow). Read together, this says the 58 translations come in 11 languages. Replace with: "The Odyssey: 24 translations in 11 languages · the library: 34 in English".

3. **Strip cell "58 translations, with the Greek beside them".** Read alone, this suggests 58 translations of each work, or 58 for the Odyssey. Replace with: "58 translations across the seventeen works".

4. **Content note: "The Odyssey includes monsters and battles, and some plays and histories in the library include violence and mature themes."** This makes the Odyssey sound milder than the old wording did ("violence and mature themes"), yet it ends with the slaughter of the suitors and the hanging of the maids. Replace with: "The Odyssey and many works in the library include violence and mature themes."

5. **FAQ "Is it free?": "Yes. Books I, II and IX are free to read in full. One purchase unlocks the other 21 books, along with the rest of the Greek library."** "Books I, II and IX" is not tied to the Odyssey, and the answer leaves out the free first sections of the library works, even though the pricing FAQ and the hero both mention them. Replace with: "Yes. Books I, II and IX of the Odyssey and the first section of every library work are free to read in full. One purchase opens the rest of the Odyssey and the library."

6. **The verb changes between "unlocks" and "opens".** The FAQ says "One purchase unlocks the other 21 books"; everywhere else the page says "opens". Use "opens" throughout.

## Optional polish

7. **Hero phone caption "The Odyssey at the top, then the shelves."** This is vague to a first-time visitor. Replace with: "The library: the Odyssey first, then five shelves of Greek works."
8. **FAQ order.** An existing reader arriving after the rename looks for "What happened to Skald: Odyssey?" first. Put it as the first FAQ, with "What is in Skald?" second.
9. **"In the European Union and Australia, W. D. Ross's Ethics and some of the Loeb Plato are not offered…"** This is accurate but sits in the middle of a counting answer, and "the Loeb Plato" is jargon to a newcomer. Move it to the end of the answer and write: "A few library translations (W. D. Ross's Ethics and some Plato translations) are not available in the European Union and Australia; the other translations of those works are."
10. **"Each has its Greek text, translations, reading notes and pictures beside the passages they illustrate."** This sentence appears almost word for word in the hero and in the library section. In the library section, cut it down to: "The first section of every library work is free."
11. **"What they made gives us more to notice"** (Generations of readers). Write "Their work gives us more to notice", which keeps the original wording.
12. **Notes paragraph.** "In the Odyssey, some notes speak to a particular translation" and "For the Odyssey, references to 52 journal articles" open two sentences in a row with almost the same phrase. Merge them: "In the Odyssey, some notes speak to a particular translation, so fresh notes can surface as you reread or change editions, and references to 52 journal articles give you somewhere to go next."
