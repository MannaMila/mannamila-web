Physics

The ball does
what a ball does.

Mila Squash starts from the ball. Flight, contact and court are modeled from published sports-ball measurements and tuned on a Quest 3 until the serve that works on court works here.

  1. S

    Spin

    Spin comes off the racket from face angle and the brush of the swing. It stays on the ball through wall and floor bounces and bends the flight through Magnus lift, so cut and topspin change where the ball lands and how it comes off the floor.

  2. A

    Air and heat

    Drag slows the ball through the air. The ball also carries a temperature that climbs as you hit it, and a warmer ball comes off the walls livelier, which is why the first serves of a session feel different from the twentieth.

  3. R

    The racket

    Your controller is a racket with a face, a frame and a sweet spot. The contact model takes face angle, swing speed, the racket's effective mass and where on the strings the ball lands. Off-center hits twist and lose pace.

Why 200 frames per second. A hard-hit ball covers 15 centimeters or more between two physics steps, several times its own diameter, and a racket face is a few millimeters thick. Check for contact once per frame and you miss hits. Mila Squash steps physics at 207 Hz, sweeps the racket's path between steps to find the exact moment and point of contact, and renders at 200 frames per second on Quest 3 so the picture keeps up with your hand. It holds that rate consistently in play.

Sound and feel

You hear the hit
before you read the card.

Every impact has its own sound: strings, front wall, side walls, back wall, floor, ceiling and the flat clang of the tin. The sounds are generated for the game rather than taken from a sample pack, a clean hit and a frame hit sound different, and the ball's note shifts slightly as it warms. Contact also fires a short haptic pulse in the controller.

Accurate enough to time a swing by ear.

What it tracks

Every serve
leaves a record.

After each serve you get a verdict against the serve rules, legal or fault, and three coaching signals: depth into the back court, tightness to the side wall, and the tempo of your motion. The shot card adds the trajectory, where the ball met the strings and the serve's numbers.

Over a session the Stats view compares your left and right service boxes, keeps a log you can step through serve by serve, and charts consistency, target accuracy, contact and legality. All of it is saved on the headset and never uploaded.

  • Verdict
  • Depth
  • Tightness
  • Tempo
  • Trajectory
  • Contact point
  • Box comparison
  • Session log
  • Consistency
  • Target accuracy

Coaching signals describe each attempt. They are training feedback, not officiating.

Where it goes from here

The serve is the start.

The engine under Serve Practice already models the whole court, the full flight of the ball and every contact. The open question is which training to build on it next, and alpha testers get a say.

Returns and rallies

Fed serves and drives to return, with the same contact model and the same card after each shot.

Targets and patterns

Target zones on the front wall and floor, length drills, and movement patterns between shots.

Coaching from the data

The headset already records contact point, speed and spin for each serve. Drills can react to your own history instead of following a fixed script.

These are directions we are weighing, not a roadmap. This build is Serve Practice only. Full matches and multiplayer are not part of it, and we are not promising them.

Questions

Before you serve.

Is this a full squash match?

No. This build is Serve Practice: serve into the box, get a legal-or-fault verdict, read the coaching on depth, tightness and tempo, and check your stats. Other drills may follow; full matches and multiplayer are not part of this build and are not promised.

How do I get in?

Mila Squash is in a private alpha on Meta Quest 3. Request access through Meta; requests are reviewed by hand, testers must be 18 or older, and the channel holds 200 people. The store release date is not set.

Does the alpha cost anything?

Nothing while the alpha runs. We have not set store pricing yet, and alpha access does not turn into a free copy or a credit at launch.

Which headset does it run on?

Meta Quest 3 with the standard Touch controllers. Quest 3S owners can join the alpha too. The 200 frames per second figure is measured on Quest 3; we have not measured Quest 3S.

How much space do I need?

Standing play in a space the size of a racket swing. You do not need a full court, and there is no artificial locomotion.

Is it comfortable in VR?

The camera never moves on its own and play is standing or stationary. Mila Squash targets a Comfortable rating.

What happens to my serve history?

It is saved in encrypted storage on your headset and never uploaded. You can clear it any time from Options → Data & Privacy. See the privacy policy.

Does the app phone home?

The Quest platform needs a connection for installation, updates and the alpha access check. The app can send crash reports and coarse usage analytics when those options are on. Your serve history is never uploaded.

Does this website use cookies?

Yes, for Google Analytics page-view counts, with advertising features turned off. The privacy policy describes what is measured and how to opt out.

Who publishes Mila Squash?

MannaMila LLC publishes Mila Squash independently. It is not affiliated with the World Squash Federation or any squash federation.
